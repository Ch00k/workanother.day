Month by month

{% explainer "month-by-month-help" beside %}

The ryczalt on a month's revenue and the ZUS contributions with the DRA that declares them both fall due on the 20th of the month after it, December included. A date landing on a Saturday or a day off work moves to the next working day.

{% if schedule.rate %}

Ryczalt at {{ schedule.rate|floatformat:"-2" }}%, rounded to whole zlote under art. 63 § 1 Ordynacji podatkowej. Contributions paid come off revenue under art. 11: social in full, health at half. A month that cannot use all of them keeps the rest available for a later one, and what no month uses is taken in the annual return instead — so these figures and the return's own can legitimately disagree.

Health contribution

{% explainer "health-help" %}

The band follows revenue accumulated from the start of the year, less social contributions paid, and a threshold crossed in a month is paid at the higher amount from that month on with no correction of earlier ones at the time.

The annual settlement then recomputes every one of the year's insured months at the band the year ends in and charges the difference, which is the provision stated here. A negative one is a refund, and it has to be claimed rather than arriving.

The bases are 60, 100 and 180 percent of the average wage in the fourth quarter of the year before, announced each January and fetched by nothing here. The wage goes in with manage.py health_contribution, which works the three out from it.

Ryczalt paid for {{ year }}

{% explainer "ryczalt-paid-help" beside %}

One transfer to the mikrorachunek podatkowy, form symbol PPE, naming the month the tax is for. Nothing is filed with it: the year's payments are declared afterwards, in PIT-28.

A payment is not a deduction, so nothing the year owes moves when one is recorded. What it changes is the balance the return settles, which is the year's own figure — taken over the whole year rather than the twelve monthly ones added up — less the ryczalt paid for its months. A negative balance is an overpayment, which the return claims back.
