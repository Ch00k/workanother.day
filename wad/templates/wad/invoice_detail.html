{% explainer "ksef-help" %}

KSeF is Poland's national e-invoicing system. An invoice sent there is issued by KSeF accepting it: the number it gives back and the link that verifies it are what make the document an invoice, and until then this is a draft nobody holds.

It is authorised with the seller's KSeF token, and both parties are identified by tax identifier rather than by name - which is why a buyer without one falls outside it.

The date on the invoice has to be the day it is sent. KSeF treats an earlier one as issued offline, which needs a certificate and a second QR code this does not produce.

Sending ends with the invoice in flight rather than accepted: KSeF processes it afterwards, so this page waits and says what came back. A rejected invoice keeps the reason in KSeF's own words and can be sent again once the cause is fixed.

{% explainer "delivery-help" %}

Art. 106gb ust. 4 requires an invoice to reach a buyer with no Polish NIP outside KSeF as well, in a manner agreed with them, because they cannot go and read it there. Sending it to KSeF is not sending it to the buyer.

The message comes from the seller's own address, under the name the invoice was issued in, and carries the document as a PDF. What it says is the contract's to settle: a contract with wording of its own is written in those words, and one without gets a short note naming the invoice and its dates.

Every attempt is kept, including the failures - a failed send is the reason an invoice is still undelivered. Sending again is allowed and is another attempt rather than a correction of the last one, which is what answers a buyer who says nothing arrived.

Nothing here says the buyer read it, or even that any server past the first one accepted it. What is recorded is that this application handed the message over and nothing objected, which is as much as SMTP can be asked.
