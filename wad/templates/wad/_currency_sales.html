{% explainer "currency-sales-help" %}

Art. 6 ust. 1c of the ryczalt act applies art. 24c PIT, which measures two differences and not one. The invoice above carries the first: what the revenue was booked at against what it was worth when the money landed. This is the second, on the money itself: what the currency was worth coming in against what it was worth going out.

The two meet at the day of the inflow and do not overlap. The inflow side uses the NBP rate of the working day before the money landed, which art. 24c ust. 4 supplies because nothing is converted on receipt. The outflow side uses the rate the kantor actually dealt at, which no table publishes, so it is entered from the confirmation along with what that confirmation is called.

A difference arises even where the currency is sold the day it arrives, because an NBP average is being compared against a dealt rate and the spread between them survives. It is small, and it is usually negative.

Each sale is a register entry of its own, dated the day of the sale and carrying this invoice's ryczalt rate. Selling a payment in parts is several sales here; a sale can never exceed what this invoice brought in, which is what keeps art. 24c ust. 8 and its lot matching out of the arithmetic.
